Biodiesel from Vegetable Oil

ශාක තෙල්වලින් ජෛව ඩීසල්

▶ Open the interactive 3D simulation

Overview and theory

Transesterification: a triglyceride (vegetable oil) reacts with methanol in the presence of a base catalyst (NaOH/KOH, as sodium methoxide) to give fatty-acid methyl esters (biodiesel) and glycerol. The oil must be dry: water causes saponification (soap). Glycerol is denser, so it settles below the biodiesel.

Apparatus and key points

Materials: filtered vegetable / waste cooking oil (100–200 mL), methanol (≈20 % of oil volume), NaOH or KOH (≈4 g per litre of oil), beakers, flask, hot plate, separating funnel, warm distilled water. Safety: methanol is toxic and flammable, lye is corrosive: goggles, gloves, lab coat, good ventilation.

Step-by-step procedure (5 steps)

  1. Filter the oil and warm it to 50–60 °C to drive off moisture.
  2. Dissolve NaOH in methanol to make sodium methoxide (caustic!).
  3. Pour the methoxide slowly into the warm oil; stir at 50–65 °C for 30–60 min.
  4. Transfer to a separating funnel and leave undisturbed for 8–24 h: glycerol (bottom) and biodiesel (top) separate.
  5. Drain the glycerol; wash the biodiesel gently with warm distilled water and let it dry.
