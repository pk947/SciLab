Archimedes’ Principle

ආකිමිඩීස් මූලධර්මය

▶ Open the interactive 3D simulation

Overview and theory

Archimedes’ principle: when a body is wholly or partly immersed in a fluid it feels an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. So the apparent loss of weight W_A − W_W equals the weight of the displaced water.

Apparatus and key points

Apparatus: solid object (metal bob or stone), spring balance, overflow (Eureka) can filled to the brim with water, small catch-beaker, digital weighing scale.

Step-by-step procedure (6 steps)

  1. Suspend the object from the spring balance and note its weight in air (W_A).
  2. Weigh the empty catch-beaker on the digital scale and record its mass (M_C).
  3. Place the beaker under the spout of the overflow can (filled to the brim) and slowly lower the object until it is fully submerged, without touching the bottom or sides.
  4. Read the new, lower weight on the spring balance (W_W).
  5. When the dripping stops, weigh the beaker with the displaced water (M_CW).
  6. Compare the apparent loss of weight (W_A − W_W) with the weight of the displaced water, (M_CW − M_C)g.
